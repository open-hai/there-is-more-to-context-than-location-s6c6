## Summary

**Paper.** Schmidt, Beigl & Gellersen, "There is more to context than location," *Computers & Graphics* 23(6):893–901, 1999 — audited from the authors' own draft PDF (MD5 verified), since the version of record is paywalled and unfetchable here.

**Verdict: partial.** The paper's conceptual machinery re-runs — the four-rule working model and six-category feature space, the sensor→cue→context→scripting architecture, the scripting semantics. Everything touching measurement is partial or blocked for a single reason: the paper reports no quantitative result at all, so a reimplementation can be built from it but never checked against it. Per-component: **3 verified, 7 partial, 5 blocked of 15 inner-loop components**, with the derived rate explicitly subordinated to the table.

**The boundary's real finding.** The outer loop here is *absent, not unreproducible*. Six human-facing claims carry the paper's argument — usability of the light-sensitive display, three interaction benefits of orientation awareness, "hardly used, if at all," the situated recordings, recognition ground truth, and the three application benefits — and not one has a described procedure. I drew the line by requiring genuine human participation: unavailable 1999 hardware stayed *inside* the inner loop as a documentation blocker, because rebuilding a Palm Pilot needs no participants, while the situated data collection went *outside*, because those recordings exist only when a person enacts the situations.

**What the arithmetic surfaced.** Two of the five mismatches came from taking the paper's own single number seriously: "about 1100 Bytes per second" makes mains-flicker detection feasible at four channels (275 Hz) and infeasible at six (183 Hz), leaving the paper's own "artificial light" cue undetermined; and Section 6's "direction of light" from several light sensors is inexpressible under Section 5.1's single-sensor cue rule — recorded as a mismatch on a *verified* row rather than hidden by the outcome.

**Deliverables** in `/mnt/session/outputs` (and pushed to `main`): `README.md`, `REPRODUCIBILITY.md`, `SOURCES.md` (34 URLs with HTTP results, four dead TEA links the authors could still fix), `UNVERIFIED.md` (18 items, each with its blocker), `verdict.json`, `instrument.json` (`protocol: null` with its reason — writing a design would mean inventing the study), and `src/` (~1,600 lines; 24 self-test checks pass, transcripts match line for line).
